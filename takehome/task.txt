===============================================================================
TASK — Rigid Flange Coupling: increase bolt count from 4 to 6
===============================================================================

CAD system      : FreeCAD 1.1.x (native files: *.FCStd)
Portable format : STEP (AP214) *.step is provided for every part and assembly,
                  so the model can also be opened in SolidWorks / Siemens NX.
Est. effort      : 2-6 hours for a professional engineer
Top-level file   : Before/input.FCStd  (the model to be changed)


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. BACKGROUND / WHY THIS CHANGE IS NEEDED
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The assembly "input.FCStd" is a rigid flange coupling that joins two in-line
shafts. In its current revision the two flange halves are bolted together with
FOUR M10 bolts arranged on a 90 mm pitch-circle diameter (PCD).

The driven machine on this line has been upgraded and the coupling must now
transmit a higher torque. Structural checking of the existing joint shows that
the four-bolt pattern no longer provides the required clamping force / shear
capacity with the SAME bolt grade (8.8) and the SAME M10 size.

Increasing the bolt diameter would require larger spot-faces and would not fit
inside the available flange outer diameter (the coupling sits inside a guard
with limited radial clearance, so the flange OD of 140 mm MUST NOT grow).

Therefore the accepted engineering solution is to KEEP the bolt size (M10, grade
8.8) and the flange OD (140 mm), and instead ADD two more bolts — going from a
4-bolt to a 6-bolt pattern, evenly distributed on the same 90 mm PCD. More bolts
on the same circle raises the total clamping force and the torque capacity of
the friction/shear joint without any change to the envelope.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. THE TASK
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Modify the flange-coupling assembly so that the two flange halves are joined by
SIX M10 bolts instead of four.

Required outcome:

  (a) The bolt pattern on BOTH flange halves changes from 4 to 6 holes, evenly
      spaced (60 deg apart) on the existing 90 mm PCD. The holes in the two
      halves must stay aligned.

  (b) SIX bolts and SIX nuts are placed in the assembly, one per hole, correctly
      seated (bolt head on one flange face, nut on the opposite face).

  (c) The change must be PARAMETRIC. The number of bolts is driven by a single
      parameter ("bolt_count") held in the model's parameter spreadsheet. The
      bolt holes (polar pattern) and the bolt/nut array must both follow that
      parameter — changing it to any sensible value must rebuild cleanly.

  (d) Nothing else about the model may change: the bore diameter (40 mm),
      keyway, hub, flange OD (140 mm), flange thickness, PCD (90 mm), bolt size
      (M10) and overall length must all stay exactly as in the "Before" model.

Deliver the result as "After/solution.FCStd" plus all referenced part files and
a STEP export, and a screenshot "After/solution.png".


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. CONSTRAINTS
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
* Flange outer diameter stays 140 mm (envelope is fixed).
* Bolt size stays M10, grade 8.8; PCD stays 90 mm.
* Bore 40 mm and the 12x8 mm keyway are unchanged.
* The six holes must be equally spaced and symmetric about the keyway axis so
  the coupling can still be assembled in the natural orientation.
* No interference between bolts, nuts and the hub/bore.
* The model must rebuild with NO errors and NO broken external references.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. WHAT "DIFFERENT CORRECT ANSWERS" LOOKS LIKE
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
There is more than one valid way to reach the result, e.g.:
  * driving the count from a spreadsheet alias vs. a document global variable;
  * a PartDesign polar pattern vs. a Draft polar array for the holes;
  * hex bolts modelled in detail vs. simplified standard-part stand-ins;
  * the 6 holes clocked with one hole on the vertical axis, OR clocked so the
    gaps straddle the keyway — both are acceptable as long as spacing is even,
    the two halves align, and symmetry about the keyway is preserved.
The verification checklist (checklist.txt) is written to accept any of these.
